# Keep transfer work private

Select an existing trusted private parent outside the extracted source, collection checkout and public or synchronized folders. Create new bundle and restore paths beneath that parent. The examples use invented filenames and contents; review the tool and its limits before applying the method to real operational data.

A source inventory is an explicit copying selection, not clearance to publish. Review ownership, licenses, secrets, personal information and embedded paths before accepting its exact bytes and recording its SHA-256 through an independent trusted channel. The tool does not import or execute selected source, and its text/name filters are not a privacy audit.

Opaque bundles preserve filenames and bytes. They do not redact, anonymize, encrypt, compress or prove application consistency. The stopped flag is your assertion: stop writers through your approved process before a real backup or restore. The tool does not inspect running processes or stop applications for you.

Keep real source trees, inventories, manifest pins, payloads, backups, restores, success summaries, failed outputs, local paths and raw diagnostics private. A success summary contains file counts, byte counts and a manifest hash; manifests and payloads disclose relative names and contents. An internal checksum detects accidental corruption but does not authenticate a replacement bundle.

Failures may leave a new partial destination. Retain and treat it as unconfirmed private material. Verification failure does not roll it back, and a retry requires a different new destination. The tool never deletes source, old backups or partial outputs.

Use one operator with stable trusted local files. The sampled identity checks do not provide an atomic snapshot, hostile-filesystem sandbox, hard I/O deadline, ACL preservation or confidentiality guarantee. Mapped drives, network/cloud folders, arbitrary mounts and actively hostile mutation are outside qualification. Follow applicable permissions and workplace policy.
